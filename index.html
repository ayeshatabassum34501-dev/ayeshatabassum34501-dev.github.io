Research Experience & Projects

The Role of Psychological Modulation and Emotion Regulation in Pain Perception among Young Adults (2026)
Supervisor: Dr. Nazia Iqbal | Tools: IBM SPSS, PSS-10, PCS, ERQ, NRS
  • Investigated the relationships between perceived stress, pain catastrophizing, emotion regulation, and pain perception in young adults using a correlational cross-sectional design.
  • Collected questionnaire-based data from 151 undergraduate students using the PSS-10, PCS, ERQ, NRS, and demographic sheet through convenience sampling.
  • Analyzed data in IBM SPSS using descriptive statistics, Cronbach's alpha, Pearson correlations, multiple regression, hierarchical moderation analysis, and independent-samples t-tests.
  • Found that perceived stress and pain catastrophizing significantly predicted pain perception, with the model explaining 41.7% of variance.
  • Authored an APA-style research paper and defended it before a 3-member departmental committee, receiving an A grade.
